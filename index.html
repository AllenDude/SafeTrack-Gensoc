Report a gender-related incident without giving your name.

An uncomfortable joke, being kept off the keyboard in a lab group, being left out of a study group — if it made the classroom feel unequal, the CCSICT GAD Committee wants to know. There is no login and no form asks who you are.

How your anonymity is protected

Nothing identifies you

We never ask for your name, student number, or email, and there is no account to sign in to. This prototype does not log IP addresses.

Photo details are removed

Screenshots and attachments are cleaned of GPS location, device model, and capture time before they are stored.

Only your code opens the case

Your SAFE-XXXX code is the only way to see the case status and message the committee. Whoever holds it can read it, so keep it private.

Check a report you made

Enter the code you received when you submitted. You can type just the four digits.

Codes can't be recovered if lost. If you no longer have yours, you can always make a new report.

Defense note: entry and privacy guarantee (WF01)

This screen operationalises the anonymity principle of RA 11313 (Safe Spaces Act) and the Data Privacy Act of 2012 (RA 10173): the system collects the minimum necessary personal data — in fact, none. Identity is replaced by a bearer-token-style case code, so the burden of confidentiality is structural rather than merely procedural.

Defense note: perceived intent capture (WF02)

The "perceived intent" field separates awareness deficits from hostility. GAD literature distinguishes between unconscious gender bias (which is best addressed through sensitivity training and curriculum redesign) and intentional harassment (which requires disciplinary escalation). Capturing the reporter's perception lets the committee route cases correctly while feeding the Unawareness Index in WF05.

Defense note: metadata stripping (WF03)

EXIF data in photos can contain GPS coordinates, device serial numbers, and timestamps — all of which can de-anonymise a reporter. The prototype simulates stripping these fields before storage, demonstrating privacy by design as required by RA 10173 §20 (security measures for personal data).

Defense note: two-way anonymous chat (WF04)

The chat channel preserves the reporter's anonymity while enabling a restorative, dialogue-based resolution rather than a purely punitive one. Because the case code is a bearer token, the student controls disclosure — this mirrors survivor-centred practice in gender-based violence response protocols.

Defense note: statistical disclosure control (WF05)

Small-cell suppression (the < 5 rule) is a standard statistical disclosure control technique: reporting counts of 1–4 in a small cohort makes individual reporters trivially re-identifiable by process of elimination. Combined with role-based aggregation for the College Admin, this implements the purpose limitation and data minimisation principles of RA 10173.

Your rights under the Safe Spaces Act (RA 11313)

Under Republic Act No. 11313 (Safe Spaces Act) and institutional GAD policies, all students are entitled to a classroom and laboratory free of gender-based sexual harassment, derogatory remarks, and systemic exclusion.

The CCSICT GAD Committee uses logged reports to identify recurring biases in laboratory groupings, facilitate faculty sensitivity workshops, and ensure non-punitive, restorative resolutions.
